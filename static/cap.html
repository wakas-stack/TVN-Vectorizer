      Size on the cap
      Area
      in

      The logo's own size: change the width and the height follows, in the logo's proportions (and the other way round). An area fits the logo inside it.

      Crown

      Measured up the front seam from the brim. The design keeps about ½ in clear at the top and bottom, so a 2.5 in crown takes a design about 1.5 in tall.

      Turn

      For a side logo: turning it 10–15° counters the slope of the cap's side, so it looks straight when worn. Which way depends on the cap and the side: sew one and check.

      Sewing starts

      Where the first stitches go. At the centre (the cap rule): line by line, the line at the middle of the design first; in each line the middle part, then outwards to the right, then to the left, so the cap is pushed away from the centre seam, not bunched at it. At the bottom: the lowest row first, its middle, then right and left.

      Machine start

      Where the needle sits when the file starts (the point you set over the cap), and where it returns at the end.

      Embroidery

      Tick Puff on each colour to raise. Puff colours sew last, the way a Wilcom 3D file does: a run down the middle of each stroke, then the machine stops for you to lay the foam, then each stroke as one dense satin (0.15 mm, up to 12 mm across) that cuts the foam clean. Letters are split into strokes; parts wider than 12 mm are sewn flat. A 30-weight thread puffs fuller. Bold, simple lettering puffs best; for anything finer, a digitizer still does it better.

      A light grid under the whole design, sewn first: holds a stiff or slippery cap (performance fabric) to its backing.

      Sparser underlay and fill, for thick caps: "less is more" on a cap.

      File

        How it is digitized for a cap: sewn from the centre outwards (right and left) and from the bottom up, so the fabric is pushed away from the middle; edge-run and tatami underlay hold the thick fabric; satin only where stitches stay under 5 mm, fill everywhere wider; travel runs under stitches still to come, else the thread is trimmed. Tajima DST, read by Tajima and Happy machines.

        Auto-digitized: good for simple logos and text, not as refined as a professional digitizer. Very small text (under 0.25 in) and fine detail need manual digitizing. Sew one cap first, before a full run.
