Reading the results

RGB and CMYK are turned into a screen colour before matching. CMYK is read as printed on coated paper with the Coated FOGRA39 profile (ISO 12647-2, the usual standard for offset printing, and Adobe's default in Europe), shown and matched as its screen colour. CMYK made for another press or paper (for example GRACoL or uncoated) can differ.

ΔE is the CIEDE2000 colour difference between your colour and the thread's screen colour. Under 1 the eye sees no difference; under 2 only a trained eye sees one side by side; under 3.5 the difference is slight; above 5 it is a different colour.

A thread's colour on screen is an approximation: dye lots, sheen and the fabric all change how it looks once stitched. Use these matches as a starting point and check them against a physical thread card before production.
